German Power Price

Germany-Luxemburg day-ahead, hourly. A quantile forecast, and what it implies for a position held in the market.

This tool predicts the hourly price of electricity in Germany, one day ahead, as a full range of plausible outcomes and translates that range into the potential loss on a position of a given size.

How this works: data, features, model, and card calculations

Data sources. Historical day-ahead prices, load forecasts, and wind/solar generation forecasts for the DE-LU bidding zone come from the ENTSO-E Transparency Platform. Unplanned power plant outages come from the EEX Group Transparency API (REMIT/UMM disclosures), covering roughly five years of outage events.

Features used by the model. The dominant driver is residual load (forecast demand minus forecast wind and solar generation). Alongside it: rolling price statistics (trailing mean/std over the past week), a lag-safe rolling spike-frequency feature (how often price has recently exceeded a volatility-based threshold, as an early-warning signal for spiky regimes), and the volume of currently unplanned dispatchable generation offline (scraped from EEX, even if with a weak signal).

Model. A gradient-boosted quantile regression model (HistGradientBoostingRegressor) is trained separately for five quantiles of the price distribution (5th, 25th, 50th, 75th, 95th percentile), then calibrated with conformal prediction. The whole pipeline is walk-forward backtested across 49 monthly out-of-sample blocks, so every prediction shown here was made without access to future data.
